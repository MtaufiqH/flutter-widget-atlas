IntrinsicHeight & IntrinsicWidth

These widgets ask the children how big they would like to be, then force them all to the largest of those sizes. That costs an extra layout pass, which is why Flutter calls them relatively expensive.

Use it for

Making a Row of cards the same height so a vertical Divider reaches top to bottom, or a Column of buttons as wide as the widest.

Watch out for

Never put one inside a list item that repeats hundreds of times. A child that cannot report an intrinsic size, such as a lazy list, throws instead.

What they do
Ask the child how big it wants to beidea-Normally a child only hears the constraints. These widgets run an extra measuring pass first, then force every child to the largest natural size.
The classic usepattern-IntrinsicHeight around a Row makes all its children the same height, so a vertical Divider between two cards reaches top to bottom.
Equal-width buttonspattern-IntrinsicWidth around a Column of buttons makes them all as wide as the widest one.
The cost
An extra layout passperformance-The subtree is laid out twice, and the Flutter docs call it relatively expensive. Nested intrinsics multiply that, so never put one inside a list item that repeats hundreds of times.
Some widgets cannot answerlimit-A child that cannot report an intrinsic size, such as a lazily built list, throws instead.
Cheaper alternatives
crossAxisAlignment: stretchalternative-In a Row, stretch already makes children fill the height the Row has, which is often all you needed.
IntrinsicHeight vs a fixed heightalternative-A SizedBox with a known height costs nothing.
Tablealternative-For a grid of equal columns, Table does it in one pass.
LayoutBuilder plus TextPainteralternative-Measure text yourself once instead of measuring the whole subtree every frame.

IntrinsicHeight(
